    Safety¶

    Safety is taken seriously, even if the race itself is not. The Head of Safety has the final say on all safety-related decisions.

    Required Safety Equipment¶

    • A seat belt, door, and helmet are required for everyone riding in the car.
    • Helmets must be DOT approved with a chin strap.
    • No batting helmets, bicycle helmets, or hard hats. Those are not legal headgear.
    • One fire extinguisher in every car, and one in the pit for every team.
    • At least one working brake light when the race starts.

    Flags¶

    Flag Meaning Action
    Red Stop immediately Pull over and stop. Repeat offenders can expect severe punishment and public embarrassment (TBD).
    Black Exit the track Go to the pits immediately.

    Pit Stops Are Mandatory

    You must make a pit stop for any of the following:

    • A flat tire
    • Causing a red flag
    • Multiple non-stops on a red flag

    Pit Area Rules¶

    • A valid driver’s license is required to be in the pit area.
    • No kids allowed in the pits.
    • No weapons of any kind.
    • Pit area is limited to one car length in width.
    • ATVs, UTVs, and golf carts are allowed for utility work only on race day.

    Recommendations¶

    These are not required, but strongly suggested:

    1. Roll bars.
    2. Designate a driver, or plan to stay and camp.
    3. Disengage the air bags.

    Conduct¶

    • Rubbing is racing, but be smart about it. This is not a demolition derby.
    • Head of Safety decisions are final.

    Vehicle Classes¶

    Cars & Trucks¶

    1. 4-cylinder vehicles only. No rotary-type engines.
    2. 2-wheel-drive vehicles only.
    3. You must have at least one working brake light when the race starts.
    4. A seat belt, door, and helmet are required for everyone riding in the car. Helmets must be DOT approved with a chin strap. No batting helmets, bicycle helmets, or hard hats.
    5. Bumpers — see Technical Specifications for full bumper rules.
    6. One fire extinguisher in every car, and one in the pit for every team.
    7. Unaltered street-tread tires only.
      No mud terrain, no all terrain, no snow tires.
    8. All rear-wheel-drive vehicles must start the race with mud flaps.

    Simple Summary

    4-cylinder, 2WD, street tires, DOT helmet, working brake light, fire extinguisher, and legal bumpers. That’s the core of it.

    Technical Specifications¶

    This page covers bumpers, radiator protectors, and transponders.

    Bumper Rules¶

    Using the original stock bumper is the easiest and preferred FRed Lobster bumper. With this option you’ll need to remove the plastic cover.

    Front and back bumpers are limited to one of the following:

    1. Original stock bumpers, front and back.
    2. A one-piece fabricated bumper no larger than 1.5″ schedule-40 pipe, or 2″ × .120 square tubing.

    No Sharp Edges

    Tuck and round anything you add that isn’t stock. Don’t have anything sticking out that could puncture a tire or pierce a panel. Bumpers must follow the contour of the car and not extend past the car’s width. If the safety inspector doesn’t like it, be prepared to cut it.

    Radiator Protectors¶

    Must be behind your bumper and no larger than 1″ square or round pipe/tube. They can’t extend past where your radiator is mounted. Stay inside the frame rails.

    (The best radiator protector is the person behind the steering wheel.)

    Transponders¶

    Each car needs two working transponders. They handle lap counting and timing during the FRed Lobster 100. Install them securely and in the proper location. Loose, hidden, backwards, or badly mounted transponders cause missed laps. If you’re unsure where they go, ask another driver or look at a returning car.

    Item Requirement
    Per car Two matching, working transponders
    Cost $20 per set (both transponders for one car)
    Assigned to A specific car. Switch cars → buy a new set

    Mud Shields

    A simple shield can help keep mud from building up on the transponders. Don’t place the shield too close, though — it may cause interference.

    Friday Testing¶

    We check all transponders on Friday during the heat races to verify they’re reading on the lap-counting system. If yours don’t register during the heats, you’ll need to replace them before Saturday’s main event.

    Save Everyone Time

    Transponder issues are one of the biggest things that slow us down on Friday, so check yours ahead of time. If they’re more than two years old, or look faded, cracked, or weathered, go ahead and change them out now.

    Race Procedures¶

    Race Teams¶

    1. Each team must have a minimum of four licensed drivers. Drivers under 18 require parent/guardian consent.
    2. The driver must have a functioning RACEceiver and respond to communications.
      Find one on Amazon or at Day Motorsports in Tyler.
    3. Team drivers must change when instructed to do so.
    4. Drivers are not allowed to drive multiple shifts in the same car.
    5. Each team must provide a safety corner flagger if it’s determined they’ll be needed.
    6. Pit area is limited to one car length in width.
    7. ATVs, UTVs, and golf carts are allowed for utility work only on race day.
    8. No weapons of any kind.
    9. Drinking is not a requirement.
    10. Teams must be registered by a predetermined date ($100). A $50 late fee applies after that date. You can pay at the Driver’s Meeting or the morning of the race.
    11. Pre-event driver’s meeting attendance is recommended and usually influences a team’s starting position.

    Driver Changes

    Drivers must switch when instructed. One driver cannot run multiple shifts in the same car.

    Penalties & Claims¶

    Claims¶

    1. Claim amount is $1,500.
    2. Claim must be made by the end of the 50th lap.
    3. A claim may only be made by an active race team member.
    4. Claim is cash in hand to the Safety Coordinator.
    5. The claimee will be advised of a claim, but not who made it.
    6. First claim is the claim.
    7. Teams can deny a claim, but the car is banned for life. The team can continue to participate.
    8. The Claim Board will decide on any discrepancies.

    Important

    If you deny a claim, the car is done for good. The team itself can still race other cars.

    Glossary¶

    Quick definitions of terms used throughout the rulebook.

    Term Definition
    A Main The main event race. Requires four individual drivers per team.
    Black Flag Signal to exit the track and go to the pits immediately.
    Claim A $1,500 cash offer to purchase another team’s car. Must be made by the end of the 50th lap.
    Claim Board Group that decides disputes related to claims.
    DOT Helmet Department of Transportation approved helmet with a chin strap. Required for all occupants.
    Head of Safety Official whose decisions on safety matters are final.
    Mud Flaps Required on all rear-wheel-drive vehicles at the start of the race.
    Pit Area Designated area for teams. Limited to one car length in width. No kids allowed.
    RACEceiver One-way radio receiver required for all drivers so they can hear race control.
    Red Flag Signal to stop immediately. Repeat non-compliance can result in severe punishment.
    Stock Bumper Original factory bumper (plastic cover removed). Preferred option.
    Street-Tread Tires Unaltered passenger car tires. Mud, all-terrain, and snow tires are prohibited.
    Transponder Electronic device used for lap counting and timing. Two working units required per car.
